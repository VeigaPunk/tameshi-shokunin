Findings from the map:

1. **The class is implemented twice with four independent differences.** `gate-line-recheck` and
   `pointer-recheck` both answer "does the content a record cites at line N sit where it says at a
   pinned revision", but differ in (i) pointer extraction (auto prose vs hand manifest), (ii) target
   line space (rendered vs raw), (iii) match predicate (span start equality vs substring-in-span),
   (iv) red arm (observed finding vs declared `expect`). Neither subsumes the other: a rendered
   pointer cannot be graded by `sed` offsets, and a manifest row cannot be discovered.
2. **Their populations are disjoint and nothing states the relation.** All 12 `pointer-recheck`
   rows target plain-text files (`corpus/SOURCES.md`, `tools/mutate-and-run.sh`, `SCORECARD.md`,

   surfaces between them (round-99 spot-check item 4 / plan amendment A8 is the routing, not a fix).
3. **Why D1 survived every neighbouring instrument.** Over the *same five files*: gate-line reports
   6 misses while quoted-line-exists reports 9/9 backed, tally-reconcile 12 of 13 pairs `unbound`
   plus 9 `uncited` with zero mismatched, and anchor-drift is green by construction (no line
   numbers). Three green neighbours and one red instrument on the same bytes is the measured shape
   of the blind spot, not an anecdote.
4. **Observation, not a claim falsifier:** `quoted-line-exists` prints its doc-sweep summary as

   `doc=` field). A record that quotes `doc=<path>` from that line cannot match. Same family,
   different defect class; reported because the overlap map is the place it would bite.

## F4 — (a) the proposed cross-check arm, executed: X1 crosswalk

Artifact: `R100CN1b-x1-crosswalk.py` (driver, sha256 `51aebc61…7d25`) and
`R100CN1b-x1-crosswalk.out`.

```
$S=/fast/scratch/hyaku/tameshi-r100/R100CN1b
git -C LANE show FREEZE:tools/gate-line-recheck.py   > $S/gate-line-recheck.py
git -C LANE show FREEZE:tools/pointer-recheck.py     > $S/pointer-recheck.py
python3 -I R100CN1b-x1-crosswalk.py --lane LANE --rev FREEZE \
  --tool $S/gate-line-recheck.py --precheck $S/pointer-recheck.py --work $S \
  --record corpus/lessons/round-95.md --record corpus/lessons/round-96.md \
  --record corpus/lessons/round-97.md --record corpus/lessons/round-98.md \
  --record corpus/lessons/round-99.md
```

Design: take gate-line's own extracted pointers (18 bound, 3 unbound skipped), render each cited
snapshot with the renderer blob at `--rev`, commit the gate sections verbatim plus the rendered
text into a scratch fixture (`git init`, toplevel asserted, fixture rev printed), emit
`tameshi-pointer-recheck-v1` rows — **expect=miss at the cited line**, **expect=hit at the observed
span**, needle = the exact rendered bytes of the quote (page spacing and inter-line newlines kept;
a single-spaced quote is *not* a substring, because a tag becomes one space beside the page's own
runs) — and let `pointer-recheck` grade them.

```
POINTER_RECHECK: rows=24 met=24 unexpected=0     X1_MAIN_RC=0
POINTER_ROW round-95-hit-84  expect=hit  measured=miss MISMATCH   X1_TRAP1_RC=1
POINTER_ROW round-95-miss-88 expect=miss measured=hit  MISMATCH   X1_TRAP2_RC=1
X1_VERDICT agree
```

24 rows = 18 hit arms + 6 miss arms (6 round-95 misses; the six round-96 pointers are cited at
their own location, so they carry no discriminating miss arm). The two trap arms forge the
expectation in each direction and both flip, so the arm is able to fail. This makes `pointer-recheck`
confirm `gate-line-recheck`'s coordinate set on the same population with an independent matcher.

Declared limits: the arm **reuses the renderer's line space**, so it cross-checks the
pointer→coordinate mapping and the line arithmetic, not the rendering (F5 shows why no raw-line
instrument can substitute); it is **start-line** evidence — `lines N-M` range-end equality stays
gate-line-only; the needle is the quote's span bytes, so a hit means "present in the span", and the
start-line identity comes from `locate()`, not from the substring test; the emitter shares
gate-line's parser by construction (mitigated only in that the needle is drawn from rendered bytes,
never from gate-line's printed numbers).

## Second-order effects and risks

- **X1's shared grammar.** The arm consumes gate-line's parser to find pointers, so a parser defect
  is invisible to it. Only the needle is independent (drawn from rendered bytes). Its real value is
  the coordinate re-derivation, and its two traps show it fails when coordinates are wrong.
- **Fixture identity.** The crosswalk fixture is ephemeral; `X1_FIXTURE` (fixture commit) and the
  renderer sha in the receipt are identity only. Rows are derived at run time, so a later round
  cannot inherit a stale pin — the arm re-derives or fails.
- **Sibling scan reads working trees.** Lane HEADs are in the receipt; a sibling that lands before
  this report is read would need the scan re-run.
- **If the arm were promoted**, it must not become a second red arm for the same population without
  a declared precedence — otherwise a renderer change turns both `gate-line` and X1 red and the
  round cannot tell a genuine miss from a renderer edit. Today X1 is an *arm*, not a gate.
