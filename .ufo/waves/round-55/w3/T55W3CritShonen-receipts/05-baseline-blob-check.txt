   466	## No-permissiveness audit recipe
   467	
   468	Goal: prove a change did not make anything pass by absence — no finding went
   469	quiet, no arm stopped firing, no claim passed on a warning line. In order:
   470	
   471	1. **Fix the revision under audit.** Line refs pin to a sha; a reader on a
   472	   differing revision re-resolves. Stamps are recomputed (`ok=56 stale=0`),
   473	   never re-typed (gates-survey-kagi.md:246-254, 420-438).
   474	2. **Capture the before-set; compare against it.** Prior rows/ledgers are the
   475	   regression fence — "prior rows 0-mismatch", "all 102 prior ledger rows +
   476	   receipts byte-stable" (verification-patterns-fleet.md:37-39).
   477	3. **Compare by byte-identity; scrub volatility only by an explicit list**
   478	   (pids, timing, paths, ASLR, decimal wobble), and keep residual line drift in
   479	   a named field (`provenance_only_line_diff`)
   480	   (verification-patterns-fleet.md:32-35).
   481	4. **Match verdicts fail-closed:** a claim passes only when the exit code equals
   482	   the expected one AND the expected ERE matches the last non-empty output line;
   483	   a verdict-shaped string followed by failure fails (`reason=verdict-not-final`)
   484	   (gates-survey-kagi.md:161-168).
   485	5. **Abstention is declared, counted, exit-neutral — and proven both ways.**
   486	   `[substrate]` rows `DEFER … reason=substrate-absent`; the guard is the
   487	   both-paths selftest; only-ABSENT/SKIP output may exit 0
   488	   (gates-survey-kagi.md:187-197).
   489	6. **Zero-work is a defect, not a green** (gates-survey-kagi.md:139-143).
   490	7. **Falsifications and defects are findings, never softened;** the verifier
   491	   catches what the author missed; post-verification changes carry their own
   492	   proof (verification-patterns-fleet.md:46-57).
   493	8. **Leave holes named as unknowns, not as green:** live verdicts unmeasured for
   494	   five of six kagi gates; the kumitate integration group not executed
   495	   (gates-survey-kagi.md:442-465; gates-survey-kumitate.md:320-331).
