### Finding (cross-seat, for L1 and R95E2) — `escaped-backslash-pipe` as specified does not discriminate

The plan and `specs/R95E2.md` name the fixture "witness cell of row C01 ends `C:\\`"
(`"C:" + chr(92)*2 + " |"`). Measured cell counts (`R95E1-backslash-discriminate.out`):

| C01 row bytes | v94 cells | v95 cells | v94 parse | v95 parse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `\| C01 \| fixture arm \| KILLED \| C:\\ \|` | 4 (`C:\\`) | 4 (`C:\`) | pass | pass |
| `\| C01 \| fixture arm \| KILLED \| C:\\\|` | 4 (`C:\|`) | 4 (`C:\`) | pass | pass |
| `\| C01 \| fixture arm\\\| KILLED \| differs \|` | 3 (`C:\| KILLED`) | 4 | `width:9:cells=3`, ids false | pass, `C01=KILLED` |

`\\|` gives v95 one cell **more** than v94; an extra cell is only benign in trailing
position, and a trailing merge is also tolerated by v94. To reproduce the routed defect
(kill rule e) the backslash pair must end a cell **left of the verdict column** (the arm
cell works). I messaged R95E2 with this table; I did not touch their file.

R95E2 confirmed the analysis and shipped the row
`| C01 | fixture arm \\| KILLED | C:\\ |` (arm-cell `\\|` plus the spec's witness
`C:\\ |` suffix). I verified those exact bytes against both counters — v94: cells
`['C01','fixture arm \| KILLED','C:\\']`, `badrows=1 width:9:cells=3`, C01 absent;
v95: cells `['C01','fixture arm \','KILLED','C:\']`, `badrows=0`, `C01=KILLED`.
Receipt: `R95E1-backslash-e2row.out`. The state therefore discriminates and keeps the
plan's row suffix.
