## 2. The tag-strip convention, and the quotes with their line numbers

The lane has two strippers in play and they disagree by a line here and there
(round 94's `R94S1-stripconv.py` measured this). I used the **round-93 convention**,
`R93S1-tagstrip.py:strip_tags` (`.ufo/waves/round-93/w1/R93S1-tagstrip.py:68-78`),
because it is the convention the round-93 committed `.txt` snapshots were built
with and the one the registry's anchor lines are quoted against:

```python
s = re.sub(r"<script\b[^>]*>.*?</script\s*>", " ", raw, flags=re.S|re.I)
s = re.sub(r"<style\b[^>]*>.*?</style\s*>", " ", s, flags=re.S|re.I)
s = re.sub(r"<!--.*?-->", " ", s, flags=re.S)
s = re.sub(r"<[^>]+>", " ", s)          # tag → SPACE
s = htmlmod.unescape(s); s = s.replace("\u00a0", " ")
lines = [ln.rstrip() for ln in s.split("\n")]
```

Applying it to my fetched bodies gives **13 890** lines for S321 and **13 221** for
S322 — the same counts as the committed round-93 `.txt` files (13 890 / 13 221), and
the same line numbers for every quote below. (A second stripper, tags → *nothing*
instead of → space, shifts some lines by ±2 and is what the round-94 `.txt` dumps
in `round-94/sources/` use; it does **not** shift any of the six quotes quoted
here, which is why the round-93 line numbers are the ones worth citing.)

Line numbers below are 1-based in my tag-stripped text; the file is the fetched
body named in each row. Each quote is verbatim (≤ 25 words) and each was located
by an exact word-sequence match, not by eyeballing.
