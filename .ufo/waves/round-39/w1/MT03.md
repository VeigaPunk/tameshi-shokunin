## What the kill does and does not show (depth)

1. The kill is on the live axis only. `stripped_agree=1` survives because `strip_ledger`
   (`measure.py:36-38`) removes command-D lines with the hardcoded `COMMAND_D` (`:17`), not with
   the contract regex, so the stripped text holds 0 command-D lines and the vacuous guard also
   counts 0/0 — `strip_guard == strip_d == (0,0)`. The stripped axis is the degenerate `0 == 0`
   case here and cannot reject this mutant; `live_agree` (`:99`) is the sole kill conjunct.
2. `r38_regex_hits=0` and `live_agree=0` are both computed from the same runner bytes
   (`:72-73`), so the "vacuous control" conjunct and the live-agreement conjunct are consistent
   with each other on real bytes: 9 `#   js-…` ledger lines, 0 `^\s*leg` lines. Passing the r38
   conjunct does not imply live agreement — only the live comparison does.
3. The mutant is rejected by arithmetic, not by an exception: regex compiles (`load_regex` at
   `:42-55` accepts any valid pattern, no anchoring/allowlist on the value at `:48`), so a
   contract naming `^\s*leg` reaches scoring and fails on `live_agree`. No rewrite of the
   predicate was performed or needed.
