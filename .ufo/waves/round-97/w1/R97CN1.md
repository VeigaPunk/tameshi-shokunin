## Step 3 — could C1 or C2 be a duplicate of an existing lane tool?

**C1: no.** Closest lane surface on SHAPE is `tools/conjunct-disposition.py`: same
`--lane/--rev` blob reads, same receipts directory family (round-93 vs round-96), same
`*_SETUP`-exit-2 convention, and the same "a receipt must move the named conjunct" idea. The
difference is the oracle: conjunct-disposition's expectations are 19 STATIC manifest rows,
while C1 imports the committed driver's `states()`/`EXPECT_EXTRA` and calls its `grade()` on
each of 50 receipts. Closest on BINDING is `tools/receipt-bytes-bind.py`: C1's
`counter_sha=` check (receipt names a 64-hex digest, blob at R must carry it) is that tool's
rule as a special case; C1 adds the per-state verdict. No lane re-derives a receipt battery
from committed bytes with an executable oracle, and `EXPECT_EXTRA` occurs nowhere outside the
tameshi lane.

**C2: no, but the mechanism is not new.** Closest lane surface is `tools/mutate-and-run.sh`:
exact single edit, `count==1` uniqueness assert before any run, `@new == @old` refused,
pristine control (red control exits 2), `KILLED/SURVIVED/INCONCLUSIVE`, and object-store
materialization of pinned legs. C2 re-implements that machinery and adds four deltas:
(i) pristine-vs-mutant differential on a FRESH per-row store with the row's own argv, judged
by a declared exit + declared token rather than a suite exit; (ii) a `limit` class with a
named mechanism; (iii) whole-run `GUARD_SETUP` refusal on a bad anchor; (iv) a checker-blob
digest pin. Closest FLEET surface is kama's `tools/round86-mutant-owners.py` (committed bytes
at `--rev`, exact patches, pristine + mutant, `killed/escaped/invalid`); its stronger rule is
attribution — the mutant's failure must name the declared owning arm — and its weaker rule is
that a bad site is a per-row `invalid` while C2 aborts the run. Neither C2's docstring nor
`plans/round-97.plan.md` cites either ancestor.
