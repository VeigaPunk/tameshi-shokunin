## 8. Calibration findings (evidence for the L1's verdict)

- **F1 — assigned arms verified externally.** All six fail-closed arms behave as the plan
  specifies, with distinct naming lines (§2). The pristine selftest is 12/12 and the
  standing sweep is `rows=6 met=6 unexpected=0` at the pinned bytes.
- **F2 — m3 is killed twice.** Frozen arm 8 (`bom-refused`, direct `decode_json`) plus
  external case (a) (CLI path through `--repo`/`--manifest`). The CLI-level refusal is
  therefore genuinely covered.
- **F3 — m1 is invisible to the standing sweep.** It is killed only by the selftest's
  comparison arms (2, 6). The sweep's stdout and exit are byte-identical to pristine, so
  the 6-row sweep alone cannot detect a comparison-layer regression; the frozen counter
  as a whole still catches m1 because its selftest half fails (10/12).
- **F4 — m2 survives the entire frozen suite.** `delimited := True` passes 12/12, leaves
  the sweep byte-identical, and changes none of (a)–(f). Only case (g) (printed integer
  present as a substring but not as a delimited token) detects it, and (g) is outside the
  frozen 12-arm selftest. This matters because the module docstring claims
  (`tools/wave-reconcile.py:25`) "Binding rules (each is a frozen selftest arm)", but the
  delimited-token rule (defined at `:88-89`, enforced at `:192` and `:203`) has **no arm**
  in `selftest()` (`:362` onward), and the `span-not-in-record` rule (enforced at `:155`
  and `:237`) also has **no arm** — case (f) shows the pristine refusal works, but nothing
  in the frozen suite would catch its removal. (I did not mutate those two paths; the
  claim is from arm coverage read at the pinned bytes, not a mutation score.)
- **F5 — the hermetic selftest does not exercise the real git reader.** Arm 11 uses
  `FixtureReader`, whose miss raises its own `rev-unresolvable-or-path-absent`; the real
  `git cat-file` failure path (case d) and the `--extra-root fleet=` resolution are
  exercised only by the standing sweep, which carries no failing comparison. Case (d)/(j)
  is the only measurement of the `git-failed:` naming line.
- **F6 — naming precision of (d).** A 40-hex rev that names no object is reported under
  the generic `git-failed:` prefix with git's fatal text; only a malformed rev (case d2)
  takes the typed `rev-unresolvable:` prefix. Both are exit 2 before any row output. If
  the plan wants a literal "unresolvable rev" naming for the 40-hex case, that is not what
  the tool prints.
