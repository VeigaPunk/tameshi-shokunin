**Definition and corpus rate.** A flaky result is "a test that exhibits both a passing and a failing result with the same code" (15 words); across the corpus "we see a continual rate of about 1.5% of all test runs reporting a" (14 words) flaky result. The post ties the rate to the fix rate: "the insertion rate is about the same as the fix rate" (11 words).

**Reach.** "Almost 16% of our tests have some level of flakiness associated with them" (13 words); equivalently "more than 1 in 7 of the tests written by our world-class engineers occasionally fail" (15 words) in a way not caused by changes to the code or tests.

**Post-submit transitions.** The CI system flags a passing test that transitions to failing so the submission can be investigated, and "about 84% of the transitions we observe from pass to fail involve a flaky test" (15 words). The lane's S026-ground entry `corpus/knowledge/repeated-control-precondition.md` already quotes this sentence from the rendered page; the feed states it in the same words.

**The default mitigation is a rerun in later CI cycles — the identification step, priced as a delay.** "build monitors typically wait for additional CI cycles to run this test again to determine whether or not the test has been broken" (23 words), "adding to the delay of identifying real problems and increasing the pool of changes that could contribute" (17 words). At project scale the arithmetic is the post's: "If 1.5% of test results are flaky, 15 tests will likely fail" (12 words) in "the average project contains 1000 or so individual tests" (9 words).

**A declared-flaky mechanism, with its latency stated.** The post's declared-flaky option is "causing it to report a failure only if it fails 3 times in a row" (15 words); "This reduces false positives, but encourages developers to ignore flakiness in their own tests" (14 words). The wait is explicit: "The breakage will not be discovered until 3 executions of the test complete, or 45 minutes" (16 words) for a 15-minute integration test, after which the post says it must still be settled "or if the test just flaked three times in a row" (11 words).

**Masking, stated as an observed habit rather than a hypothesis.** "It is quite common to ignore legitimate failures in flaky tests due to the high number of false-positives" (18 words), generalised as "It is human nature to ignore alarms when there is a history of false signals coming from a system" (19 words). The quarantine strategy is named together with what it hides: "A tool that monitors the flakiness of tests and if the flakiness is too high, it automatically quarantines the test" (20 words), which "could easily mask a real race condition or some other bug in the code being tested" (16 words).

**The publisher's own direction.** "we are seeing promising correlations with features that should enable us to identify a flaky result accurately" (17 words) without re-running the test — an aspiration in the post, not a measured result.

## What is inferred

- **Inference — the false-red prior.** If about 84% of observed pass→fail transitions in a large corpus involve a flaky test, one red observation in that corpus is weak evidence of a defect, and a verdict rule run on a corpus with that transition rate would carry a comparable false-red share unless it states an attempt budget or an independent witness. Whether this lane's own legs — single-run probes of deterministic fixtures, counted by `corpus/QUALITY.md` criterion 82 as killed when a completed run fails a declared check — have any such share is not measured here.
- **Inference — rerun as deferred identification.** The post's rerun is the identification step ("to determine whether or not the test has been broken"), deferred to later CI cycles; the cost the post names is the delay. Its declared-flaky option needs three consecutive failures before it reports one, and even then the post says the question remains whether the test is broken or "just flaked three times in a row": an attempt count bounds the wait, not the determination.
- **Inference — the discovery-latency term.** Attempts multiply test duration: the post's 15-minute test becomes 45 minutes at three attempts. A per-attempt rerun budget is therefore priced in executions × duration, and that term is bounded by the test's runtime, not by the flake rate.
- **Inference — suppression without a stated floor converts a known failure into an ignored one.** Because developers are reported to ignore legitimate failures among flaky ones, a cadence that suppresses red builds without a stated detection floor moves a real failure into the ignored class. The floor is the condition under which the cadence is being paid for at all, so it belongs in the claim, not in the report.
- **Inference — a prior on the lane's own variance.** The same figure is a prior on how much of the lane's measured run-to-run variance is worth chasing: in a large corpus a red that does not reproduce is the expected case, which is why the lane's F3 repeated-control entry treats one clean control as finite-sample agreement rather than as proof of determinism.

- The post's mitigation strategies are reported, not adopted: no claim is made that quarantine, declared-flaky flags or automatic reruns transfer to this lane's corpus.
